The departure of Xherdan Shaqiri (retired July 2024, 125 caps, 32 goals) is the tournament's most significant unseen loss for the Swiss. Predicted group goals scored: 7. Goalkeeper · Borussia Dortmund
Gregor Kobel (replacing retired Sommer)

Yakin has distributed his creative responsibilities across Ndoye, Vargas, and Michel Aebischer, but Switzerland unquestionably miss his ability to produce something out of nothing in tight games.

Analyst Verdict
Switzerland are the best team not to make the Round of 16 in this tournament. They win a tough Group B that contains a co-host (Canada), drop only the minimum points necessary (winning the group on 9 points), and then face Belgium in arguably the tournament's most one-sided-in-terms-of-individual-quality R32 match. Embolo's 2 group-stage goals make him Switzerland's leading scorer. Xhaka's leadership and Akanji's defensive reading make the Swiss extremely hard to beat, but Belgium's attacking quality — particularly De Bruyne if fit — is a level above anything Switzerland's defence has faced. The 1–2 exit is a hard-luck story; this Swiss team deserves more than a first-round knockout exit.

The confirmed Spain/France vs Argentina/England pathway split is the most important structural input. Ensures the predicted final is the ideal matchup.
The PyCoach's blog approach used a Python simulation trained entirely on historical match data — statistically rigorous but blind to the current moment. It would have treated Brazil with Rodrygo and without him identically, and had no way to model Haaland's 16-goal qualifying explosion or Yamal's emergence as a world-class player in 2024–25.

Key deviations from the blog methodology: Norway rated as top-8 threat (historical data severely underweights a team absent for 28 years). Brazil rated lower than #7 ranking suggests (new manager adjustment). Spain rated #1 overall despite France's official ranking. Messi's Argentina given strong form weighting despite injury concerns. Bracket-path predictions take precedence over pure Elo.
